## Artist Statement

*Inner Landscapes* is a series of dynamic paintings that explores emotion as a hidden structure unfolding across sound and image over time. Each work invites viewers to experience emotion through evolving patterns of color, motion, and texture. Recognizable traces of a reference image remain within the abstract marks, connecting an image we can identify with a feeling we interpret.

Inspired by calm, anxiety, nostalgia, grief, and joy, the series investigates how emotional meaning can emerge through associations between music and visual form. Each piece begins with a reference image selected to represent an emotional state. Language model assistance recommends candidate images based on visual composition, atmosphere, and semantic associations, after which the artist selects the final reference image. Music gives this visual world a changing source of movement and feeling.

The titles establish a starting point rather than a fixed response. Color, scale, and the pace of accumulation leave room for viewers to form their own emotional associations. Mathematics, machine learning, computer vision, and stochastic processes serve as creative media through which these associations take visible form.

## Technical Grounding

The series builds on the coordinate network of Chapter 4 and the random-walk painter of Chapter 5. The network learns a color rule from image location and current music features. Three zoom targets connect musical input with several visual scales. Fourier features and edge-weighted training help preserve image structure.

During rendering, walkers place strokes on an accumulating canvas. The network supplies their colors; music intensity and local image-region controls influence movement and appearance. Each work uses its own reference image, audio, checkpoint, and visual settings. The implementation uses a $512\times512$ training grid, a $1080\times1080$ rendering canvas, and a 30-frame-per-second target.

These details explain how the works were made. The equations and implementation are developed in the Education chapters; here the focus is on what the process allows the artist to express.
